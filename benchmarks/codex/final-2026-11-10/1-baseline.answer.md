**Decision: revise before production approval.** The proposed fields do not collectively guarantee five serving replicas, completed requests, or database durability. The following recommendations distinguish documented behavior from configuration choices and validation obligations. Citations refer to paths beneath the supplied `docs/` directory.

1. **PDB protection depends on the disruption.** During **node failure**, `minAvailable: 5` cannot preserve five replicas: involuntary disruptions cannot be prevented, although they count against the budget. During **node drain**, eviction-based tooling respects the budget and retries rejected evictions; with six healthy matching Pods, one eviction is permitted. Direct deletion bypasses this protection. During a **Deployment rollout**, the controller is not constrained by the PDB. These distinctions are explicit in [concepts/workloads/pods/disruptions.md, lines 99–145](docs/concepts/workloads/pods/disruptions.md#L99-L145).

   Proposed `maxUnavailable: 2` permits rollout availability to fall to four. Recommend `maxUnavailable: 1`, `maxSurge: 1`, with capacity for the extra Pod; alternatively choose zero unavailable for stricter rollout behavior. These are rollout controls, not guarantees against concurrent failures ([concepts/workloads/controllers/deployment.md, lines 1185–1211](docs/concepts/workloads/controllers/deployment.md#L1185-L1211)).

2. **Spread deliberately, with an explicit outage tradeoff.** Configure a matching Pod selector, `topologyKey: topology.kubernetes.io/zone`, and `maxSkew: 1`, targeting two replicas per zone. `topologyKey` groups nodes by label value. With `DoNotSchedule`, skew bounds the difference between the target domain’s matching-Pod count and the global minimum; violating placements remain pending. `ScheduleAnyway` instead prefers placements reducing skew without enforcing that bound ([concepts/scheduling-eviction/topology-spread-constraints.md, lines 82–130](docs/concepts/scheduling-eviction/topology-spread-constraints.md#L82-L130)).

   Recommend strict spreading only with an agreed degraded-zone recovery policy. If `minDomains: 3` is configured and fewer than three domains remain eligible, the global minimum becomes zero, potentially blocking replacements in surviving zones. Without it, the default minimum-domain count is one. Actual outage behavior depends on eligible nodes and domain visibility; removal does not guarantee continued balance ([same document, lines 107–126](docs/concepts/scheduling-eviction/topology-spread-constraints.md#L107-L126), [607–623](docs/concepts/scheduling-eviction/topology-spread-constraints.md#L607-L623)). Losing a zone containing two replicas immediately leaves only four.

3. **Separate startup, readiness, and liveness.** Use a startup probe whose `failureThreshold × periodSeconds` covers measured worst-case initialization. Choose readiness to indicate ability to serve requests, including necessary database dependencies; choose liveness for failures that restarting can resolve. These endpoint semantics require application design ([tasks/configure-pod-container/configure-liveness-readiness-startup-probes.md, lines 311–318](docs/tasks/configure-pod-container/configure-liveness-readiness-startup-probes.md#L311-L318)).

   Other probes remain disabled until startup succeeds, and their initial-delay clocks begin afterward. Threshold-exhausting startup/liveness failures trigger container termination and restart behavior; readiness failure leaves the container running and removes it from matching Service endpoints. That stops ordinary Service routing, not necessarily existing requests ([concepts/workloads/pods/pod-lifecycle.md, lines 479–497](docs/concepts/workloads/pods/pod-lifecycle.md#L479-L497); [probe configuration, lines 398–422](docs/tasks/configure-pod-container/configure-liveness-readiness-startup-probes.md#L398-L422)).

4. **Thirty seconds is a deadline, not a completion guarantee.** The grace-period countdown begins **before** `preStop`; the hook consumes that budget ([reference/kubernetes-api/workload-resources/pod-v1.md, lines 1006–1008](docs/reference/kubernetes-api/workload-resources/pod-v1.md#L1006-L1008)). Normally, the hook runs before the runtime sends SIGTERM to the main process. A still-running hook can receive a one-off two-second extension; remaining processes receive SIGKILL when grace expires ([concepts/workloads/pods/pod-lifecycle.md, lines 591–639](docs/concepts/workloads/pods/pod-lifecycle.md#L591-L639)).

   Endpoint updates occur concurrently: terminating endpoints are not immediately removed, but become `ready: false`; `serving` supports draining decisions. Implement application shutdown and measure hook time, longest accepted request, and routing propagation before selecting grace. The docs establish neither instantaneous traffic cessation nor completion within thirty seconds ([same document, lines 612–639](docs/concepts/workloads/pods/pod-lifecycle.md#L612-L639)).

5. **Set realistic resource requests and limits.** CPU requests influence scheduling and CPU allocation under contention; they are not CPU ceilings. Containers may exceed requests when resources permit. CPU limits enforce throttling, potentially extending startup and request latency. Memory limits operate reactively through OOM killing rather than CPU-style throttling ([concepts/configuration/manage-resources-containers.md, lines 27–53](docs/concepts/configuration/manage-resources-containers.md#L27-L53), [230–265](docs/concepts/configuration/manage-resources-containers.md#L230-L265)).

   Configure CPU requests for every relevant container. Utilization-based HPA divides CPU usage by requests; if some containers lack the relevant request, Pod CPU utilization is undefined and HPA takes no action for that metric. Sizing therefore affects both placement and scaling decisions ([tasks/run-application/horizontal-pod-autoscale.md, lines 89–102](docs/tasks/run-application/horizontal-pod-autoscale.md#L89-L102)).

6. **Expect conservative HPA decisions.** The raw calculation is rounded-up current replicas multiplied by current/desired metric, with default tolerance 0.1. Missing-metric Pods are initially set aside; recalculation assumes 100% of target during downscale and 0% during upscale. Not-yet-ready CPU Pods are also set aside and conservatively treated as zero during a prospective upscale. Reversed direction or a ratio within tolerance cancels scaling ([tasks/run-application/horizontal-pod-autoscale.md, lines 135–197](docs/tasks/run-application/horizontal-pod-autoscale.md#L135-L197)).

   Readiness handling uses configurable defaults of thirty seconds for initial readiness delay and five minutes for CPU initialization. Downscale stabilization selects the highest recommendation within the default five-minute window. Reported utilization excludes these conservative adjustments, explaining apparent disagreement with scaling ([same document, lines 167–178](docs/tasks/run-application/horizontal-pod-autoscale.md#L167-L178), [199–218](docs/tasks/run-application/horizontal-pod-autoscale.md#L199-L218)).

7. **Replace default allow with tested allowlists.** Apply namespace-wide default-deny ingress and egress, then permit approved callers to the API port, API access to the database port, and required operational dependencies. Isolation is directional and independent. Policies combine additively: a broad allow cannot be overridden by adding a deny policy. Both source egress and destination ingress must permit a Pod-to-Pod connection; replies are implicitly allowed ([concepts/services-networking/network-policies.md, lines 51–81](docs/concepts/services-networking/network-policies.md#L51-L81), [210–259](docs/concepts/services-networking/network-policies.md#L210-L259)).

   Explicitly allow DNS to the actual cluster resolver, including its required UDP/TCP ports; the documented DNS Service uses both on port 53 ([tasks/administer-cluster/dns-debugging-resolution.md, lines 158–173](docs/tasks/administer-cluster/dns-debugging-resolution.md#L158-L173)). Enforcement-capable networking is a prerequisite: merely creating policies has no effect without it ([network-policies.md, lines 45–49](docs/concepts/services-networking/network-policies.md#L45-L49)).

8. **Reject base64 Secrets in Git as protection.** Base64 obscures values; readers can recover credentials ([concepts/configuration/secret.md, lines 292–307](docs/concepts/configuration/secret.md#L292-L307)). Remove recoverable production credentials from repository manifests and select a controlled delivery mechanism.

   Enable encryption at rest: Secrets are otherwise stored unencrypted in etcd. Configure least-privilege RBAC and restrict container access. Audit Pod-creation permissions, including indirect creation through Deployments: these can expose any Secret in that namespace even without direct Secret-reading permission. Encryption of cluster storage does not protect repository copies ([same document, lines 35–48](docs/concepts/configuration/secret.md#L35-L48)).

9. **Separate PV reclamation from PVC retention.** Changing a StorageClass is not a retroactive change to existing PV policies. Dynamically provisioned PVs inherit its policy; inspect and explicitly edit existing PVs when necessary. The default is `Delete` ([concepts/storage/storage-classes.md, lines 126–135](docs/concepts/storage/storage-classes.md#L126-L135); [concepts/storage/persistent-volumes.md, lines 203–210](docs/concepts/storage/persistent-volumes.md#L203-L210)).

   After PVC deletion, `Retain` preserves the released PV and data for manual reclamation; supported `Delete` implementations remove both PV and backing storage. In-use protection delays PVC removal ([persistent-volumes.md, lines 123–136](docs/concepts/storage/persistent-volumes.md#L123-L136), [180–209](docs/concepts/storage/persistent-volumes.md#L180-L209)). If the database uses a StatefulSet, deletion/scaling retains template PVCs by default. Optional `whenDeleted`/`whenScaled: Delete` changes that, subject to the documented feature prerequisite ([concepts/workloads/controllers/statefulset.md, lines 391–423](docs/concepts/workloads/controllers/statefulset.md#L391-L423)). Recommend explicit retention for database storage.

10. **Prioritize rollout and validation.** First, secure credential delivery, verify encryption/RBAC, and inspect database PV/PVC retention. Second, validate policy enforcement and DNS/database allowlists. Third, measure startup and shutdown, configure probes/resources, align rollout availability, and test zone placement. Finally, exercise drain, rollout, zone loss, missing metrics, and database recovery under production-like load.

   Approval must depend on measured limits the docs do not establish: **request completion before shutdown**, **surviving-zone capacity and replacement scheduling**, **CPU scaling’s ability to meet latency targets**, and **database recovery/durability**. Relevant documented boundaries are forced termination ([pod-lifecycle.md, lines 619–639](docs/concepts/workloads/pods/pod-lifecycle.md#L619-L639)), topology limitations ([topology-spread-constraints.md, lines 607–623](docs/concepts/scheduling-eviction/topology-spread-constraints.md#L607-L623)), HPA damping ([horizontal-pod-autoscale.md, lines 183–218](docs/tasks/run-application/horizontal-pod-autoscale.md#L183-L218)), and reclamation semantics—not recovery guarantees ([persistent-volumes.md, lines 180–210](docs/concepts/storage/persistent-volumes.md#L180-L210)).
