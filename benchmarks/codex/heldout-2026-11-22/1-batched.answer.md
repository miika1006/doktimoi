Approve this design conditionally. Kubernetes supplies scheduling, admission and cleanup mechanisms, but these documents do not guarantee exactly-once reporting, immediate capacity reclamation or consistent configuration throughout a run. The dedicated-node requirement also needs clarification before rollout.

1. **CronJob execution is approximate.** A schedule can produce two Jobs or none; reporting must therefore be idempotent. `concurrencyPolicy: Allow` permits overlap; `Forbid` skips an occurrence while the previous Job remains unfinished; `Replace` replaces that Job. These policies apply only within one CronJob, not across teams. [Sources: docs/concepts/workloads/controllers/cron-jobs.md, lines 128–143 and 211–217](docs/concepts/workloads/controllers/cron-jobs.md:128).

   Choose `Forbid` if overlapping reports are unacceptable, plus an explicit lateness budget using `startingDeadlineSeconds`. This deadline controls Job creation, not execution duration. More than 100 missed schedules prevents creation; setting the deadline restricts the missed-schedule counting window. `Forbid` skips count as misses. Values below ten seconds may prevent scheduling because the controller checks every ten seconds. Set `.spec.timeZone`, for example `Etc/UTC`; otherwise the controller manager’s local time zone applies. [Source: same document, lines 109–126, 180–186 and 228–240](docs/concepts/workloads/controllers/cron-jobs.md:109).

2. **Bound retries and runtime separately.** With `restartPolicy: OnFailure`, failed containers restart within their Pod; with `Never`, a failed Pod is replaced by the Job controller. Applications must tolerate both replacement and incomplete previous output. `backoffLimit` defaults to six, with exponentially delayed replacements. Failure accounting includes failed Pods and, for `OnFailure`, container retries in Pending or Running Pods. Reaching either retry count fails the Job. [Source: docs/concepts/workloads/controllers/job.md, lines 318–342 and 359–374](docs/concepts/workloads/controllers/job.md:318).

   Set Job-level `activeDeadlineSeconds` to bound total Job duration across Pods. **The active deadline takes precedence over remaining retries**; reaching it terminates running Pods and produces `DeadlineExceeded`. Retry exhaustion can still fail the Job earlier. Neither mechanism automatically restarts a failed Job. `ttlSecondsAfterFinished` only makes finished Jobs eligible for cascading cleanup after the selected interval; it is not a runtime limit. [Source: same document, lines 623–663, 741–750 and 770–775](docs/concepts/workloads/controllers/job.md:623).

3. **Combine taints with required node affinity.** Label batch nodes, taint them—for example, `dedicated=batch:NoSchedule`—and give batch Pods the matching toleration plus required affinity to that label. A toleration permits placement but does not confine a Pod; required affinity supplies that constraint. Preferred affinity is insufficient. [Sources: docs/concepts/scheduling-eviction/taint-and-toleration.md, lines 181–191](docs/concepts/scheduling-eviction/taint-and-toleration.md:181); [docs/concepts/scheduling-eviction/assign-pod-node.md, lines 105–118](docs/concepts/scheduling-eviction/assign-pod-node.md:105).

   `NoSchedule` leaves existing Pods running. `NoExecute` evicts intolerant Pods and can time-limit tolerated residency with `tolerationSeconds`. Enforce who may add tolerations or bypass scheduling with `nodeName`; the latter bypasses `NoSchedule`. Affinity also does not evict Pods after label changes. [Sources: taint document, lines 65–71 and 97–110](docs/concepts/scheduling-eviction/taint-and-toleration.md:65); [affinity document, lines 116–118](docs/concepts/scheduling-eviction/assign-pod-node.md:116).

4. **Use lower batch priority, but resolve placement eligibility first.** Create cluster-scoped PriorityClasses with higher numeric priority for interactive services and reference them through `priorityClassName`. Give interactive Pods `PreemptLowerPriority`; batch can use `Never` to prevent it preempting other workloads. `Never` does not protect batch from being preempted. [Source: docs/concepts/scheduling-eviction/pod-priority-preemption.md, lines 51–57, 103–127 and 151–154](docs/concepts/scheduling-eviction/pod-priority-preemption.md:51).

   Preemption works only where removing lower-priority Pods would make the incoming Pod schedulable. Thus, if interactive Pods cannot tolerate batch-node taints, evicting batch cannot give them that capacity. Either retain strict exclusivity and provide interactive capacity separately, or authorize selected interactive services there. Victims receive graceful termination, delaying reclamation. PDB protection is best effort and may be violated. [Source: same document, lines 187–194 and 216–238](docs/concepts/scheduling-eviction/pod-priority-preemption.md:187).

5. **Quota is admission control, not reserved capacity.** For `requests.cpu` and `requests.memory` quotas, every incoming container needs corresponding requests; limit quotas require corresponding limits. Configure explicit values or suitable defaults. Creation or updates exceeding quota fail with HTTP 403; they do not merely enter a scheduling queue. Quota changes do not affect already-created resources, including running Pods. Verify ResourceQuota admission is enabled. Aggregate namespace quotas can exceed cluster capacity, so quota allowance does not establish available compute. [Source: docs/concepts/policy/resource-quotas.md, lines 36–41, 74–87 and 462–469](docs/concepts/policy/resource-quotas.md:36).

6. **Choose configuration consistency deliberately.** Ordinary ConfigMap volume projections update eventually. Delay can reach the kubelet sync period plus cache propagation delay, depending on its change-detection strategy. Environment variables do not update automatically and require Pod restart; `subPath` mounts receive no updates. File projection does not establish that the reporting application rereads or safely applies changed settings. [Source: docs/concepts/configuration/configmap.md, lines 191–208](docs/concepts/configuration/configmap.md:191).

   For repeatable reports, prefer a selected immutable configuration version per run. `immutable: true` prevents changes to `data` and `binaryData` and cannot be reversed. Deleting and recreating the ConfigMap does not refresh existing mount points; the docs recommend recreating those Pods. [Source: same document, lines 299–328](docs/concepts/configuration/configmap.md:299).

7. **Memory-pressure eviction is not simply “lowest priority first.”** After node-level reclamation fails, kubelet ranks Pods by whether usage exceeds requests, then priority, then usage relative to requests. BestEffort and Burstable Pods exceeding requests are evicted before Guaranteed and Burstable Pods below requests. QoS names describe likely outcomes; kubelet does not directly rank by QoS class. [Source: docs/concepts/scheduling-eviction/node-pressure-eviction.md, lines 318–342](docs/concepts/scheduling-eviction/node-pressure-eviction.md:318).

   Node-pressure eviction honors neither PDBs nor the Pod’s configured `terminationGracePeriodSeconds`. Soft eviction uses the kubelet’s eviction grace configuration; hard thresholds cause immediate shutdown with zero grace. Batch checkpointing must tolerate that distinction from scheduler preemption. [Source: same document, lines 26–36 and 185–211](docs/concepts/scheduling-eviction/node-pressure-eviction.md:26).

8. **Restricted requires compliant Pods and functioning admission.** Enable/verify PodSecurity admission and label namespaces `pod-security.kubernetes.io/enforce=restricted`, preferably pinning `enforce-version=v1.32`. Enforce rejects noncompliant Pod creation; audit records violations through annotations without blocking; warn returns user warnings, including for Job templates. [Sources: docs/reference/access-authn-authz/admission-controllers.md, lines 714–723](docs/reference/access-authn-authz/admission-controllers.md:714); [docs/reference/labels-annotations-taints/_index.md, lines 2369–2413 and 2434–2448](docs/reference/labels-annotations-taints/_index.md:2369).

   Restricted includes all Baseline controls, restricts volume types, requires non-root execution and forbids explicit UID zero. Linux requirements include `allowPrivilegeEscalation: false`, explicit permitted seccomp profiles, dropping `ALL` capabilities and adding back only `NET_BIND_SERVICE`. These apply across relevant regular, init and ephemeral containers, with documented OS exceptions. “Everywhere” needs infrastructure compatibility checks: the tutorial demonstrates restricted violations in system workloads. [Sources: docs/concepts/security/pod-security-standards.md, lines 299–451 and 495–504](docs/concepts/security/pod-security-standards.md:299); [docs/tutorials/security/cluster-level-pss.md, lines 144–171](docs/tutorials/security/cluster-level-pss.md:144).

9. **Disable unnecessary token automounting.** Pods normally receive credentials for their assigned ServiceAccount, including `default`; modern tokens are short-lived, rotating projected tokens. Set `automountServiceAccountToken: false` in the Job’s Pod template and on team ServiceAccounts as a default. Pod settings override ServiceAccount settings, so review overrides and explicitly supplied credentials separately. [Sources: docs/concepts/security/service-accounts.md, lines 141–156](docs/concepts/security/service-accounts.md:141); [docs/tasks/configure-pod-container/configure-service-account.md, lines 67–100](docs/tasks/configure-pod-container/configure-service-account.md:67).

10. **Prioritized rollout and validation checklist.** First, resolve exclusive-node versus interactive-overflow policy; test required affinity, tolerations and rejected unauthorized placement. Second, inventory application and infrastructure compliance using restricted warn/audit, then enable enforce and disable unnecessary token mounts. Third, configure resource requests, limits, quotas and PriorityClasses; test quota rejection and interactive preemption. Fourth, select CronJob overlap/lateness policy, Job retry/runtime bounds, configuration versioning and cleanup retention; exercise failures and missed schedules.

   Acceptance must validate at least four limits the documents do not guarantee: duplicate-safe report output, bounded interactive recovery latency, application configuration reload consistency, and checkpoint recovery after immediate eviction. Measure memory requests and pressure behavior under representative load. These tests follow directly from documented duplicate execution, preemption delay, eventual projection and zero-grace eviction.
