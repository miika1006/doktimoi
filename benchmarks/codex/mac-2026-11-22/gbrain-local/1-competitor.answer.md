**Decision memo — revise before production rollout.** The design combines useful Kubernetes controls, but they do not establish end-to-end availability, request completion, or database durability. The recommendations below distinguish documented behavior from configuration choices and validation prerequisites. All cited paths are within the supplied `docs` corpus.

1. **The PDB does not guarantee five replicas in all three cases.** Node failure is involuntary: PDBs cannot prevent it, although unavailable Pods count against the budget. During an Eviction-API-based drain, `minAvailable: 5` permits one disruption when six Pods are healthy; subsequent evictions can block until replacements become healthy. Direct deletion bypasses this protection. Deployment rolling updates count against the budget but are not constrained by it.  
   Recommend `maxUnavailable: 0, maxSurge: 1`, with capacity for the surge. `maxUnavailable: 1` also aligns with a five-of-six rollout floor; the proposed value of two permits only four available. Neither configuration prevents simultaneous infrastructure failures. Sources: [disruptions.md, lines 99–136](/private/tmp/doktimoi-bench-1-competitor-sWyioT/docs/concepts/workloads/pods/disruptions.md:99); [deployment.md, lines 1183–1211](/private/tmp/doktimoi-bench-1-competitor-sWyioT/docs/concepts/workloads/controllers/deployment.md:1183).

2. **Explicitly configure zone spreading.** Use the API’s label selector with `topologyKey: topology.kubernetes.io/zone` and `maxSkew: 1`, after verifying node labels. `topologyKey` groups nodes with identical label values into domains. With `DoNotSchedule`, skew limits the difference between the target domain’s matching Pod count and the global minimum; `ScheduleAnyway` merely prefers placements reducing skew.  
   Six replicas can therefore target two per zone, but losing that zone leaves four—already below five. Replacement scheduling depends on eligible domains and capacity. With `minDomains: 3`, fewer eligible domains makes the global minimum zero and can block replacements. Omitting it behaves as one; softer spreading favors recovery capacity over strict balance. Scheduling constraints do not guarantee continued balance after removals. Sources: [topology-spread-constraints.md, lines 78–130 and 605–623](/private/tmp/doktimoi-bench-1-competitor-sWyioT/docs/concepts/scheduling-eviction/topology-spread-constraints.md:78).

3. **Use startup protection, readiness for admission, and narrowly scoped liveness.** Configure a startup probe with a failure budget covering measured initialization time. Until it succeeds, other probes are disabled; their initial-delay clocks begin afterward. Readiness should indicate ability to serve requests, including necessary database availability. A readiness failure removes the Pod from matching Service endpoints without restarting its container. Liveness should detect conditions that warrant restart, such as an unrecoverable application hang; using database availability for liveness risks unnecessary restarts. Startup or liveness failures reaching their threshold trigger container restart under the restart policy. Probe endpoints and thresholds are application choices. Sources: [pod-lifecycle.md, lines 479–497 and 550–557](/private/tmp/doktimoi-bench-1-competitor-sWyioT/docs/concepts/workloads/pods/pod-lifecycle.md:479); [configure-liveness-readiness-startup-probes.md, lines 351–367 and 398–420](/private/tmp/doktimoi-bench-1-competitor-sWyioT/docs/tasks/configure-pod-container/configure-liveness-readiness-startup-probes.md:351).

4. **Thirty seconds is a deadline, not a request-completion guarantee.** Deletion records the grace period before local shutdown. The kubelet executes `preStop` before requesting TERM, so hook execution consumes the same shutdown budget. A still-running hook can receive a small, one-off two-second extension. The runtime normally signals process 1 with SIGTERM, although image stop-signal settings may apply; remaining processes receive SIGKILL when grace expires.  
   Endpoint updates happen concurrently with shutdown. Terminating EndpointSlice entries are not immediately removed, but have `ready: false` for regular traffic; `serving` supports draining decisions. Implement signal handling and connection draining, then size grace for hook time plus outstanding requests. These mechanisms do not promise every long request completes. Source: [pod-lifecycle.md, lines 567–639](/private/tmp/doktimoi-bench-1-competitor-sWyioT/docs/concepts/workloads/pods/pod-lifecycle.md:567).

5. **Set realistic resource requests and tested limits.** CPU requests inform scheduling and form the denominator for utilization-based HPA; they are not maximum consumption. Containers may exceed requests when resources are available. CPU limits enforce throttling, potentially affecting latency. Memory limits use reactive OOM enforcement: exceeding a limit can lead to termination, rather than CPU-style throttling or necessarily immediate killing.  
   If relevant CPU requests are missing from some containers, Pod CPU utilization is undefined and HPA takes no action for that metric. Inspect effective specifications: a limit without a request can become the request when no admission default supplies one. Sources: [manage-resources-containers.md, lines 18–66](/private/tmp/doktimoi-bench-1-competitor-sWyioT/docs/concepts/configuration/manage-resources-containers.md:18); [horizontal-pod-autoscale.md, lines 89–102](/private/tmp/doktimoi-bench-1-competitor-sWyioT/docs/tasks/run-application/horizontal-pod-autoscale.md:89).

6. **Expect HPA decisions to differ from the raw ratio.** The basic calculation rounds up current replicas multiplied by current/desired metric value. Failed and deleting Pods are excluded; missing metrics and qualifying not-yet-ready CPU Pods are set aside. Missing metrics are conservatively assumed at 100% of target during downscale and zero during upscale. Not-yet-ready Pods are also assumed at zero when damping upscale. A recalculated ratio reversing direction or falling within tolerance produces no scaling.  
   Default readiness-delay and CPU-initialization windows are 30 seconds and five minutes. Downscale stabilization defaults to five minutes, selecting the highest recommendation within that window. Status utilization can show the original average despite these adjustments. Source: [horizontal-pod-autoscale.md, lines 130–218](/private/tmp/doktimoi-bench-1-competitor-sWyioT/docs/tasks/run-application/horizontal-pod-autoscale.md:130).

7. **Replace default allow with explicit traffic permissions.** Establish ingress and egress isolation for API and database Pods, then allow approved callers to the API port, API egress to the database port, and corresponding database ingress. Add only required operational dependencies and DNS access to the cluster’s actual resolver. Service-name resolution depends on DNS; a database-port exception alone is insufficient.  
   Isolation is directional. Policies combine additively: a broad allow cannot be overridden by another deny policy. Both source egress and destination ingress must permit a Pod-to-Pod connection; response traffic is implicitly allowed. A NetworkPolicy-capable networking implementation is mandatory—creating accepted policy objects alone has no enforcement effect. Sources: [network-policies.md, lines 45–81 and 105–122](/private/tmp/doktimoi-bench-1-competitor-sWyioT/docs/concepts/services-networking/network-policies.md:45); [dns-pod-service.md, lines 15–26](/private/tmp/doktimoi-bench-1-competitor-sWyioT/docs/concepts/services-networking/dns-pod-service.md:15).

8. **Base64 provides no confidentiality.** Reject base64-encoded credentials in Git as the protection mechanism: the documentation demonstrates decoding and explicitly calls encoded credentials “obscured but not secret.” Enable encryption at rest for Secrets, which otherwise reside unencrypted in etcd, and apply least-privilege RBAC and container access.  
   Restrict workload-creation permissions too. Someone authorized to create Pods—or indirectly Deployments—in a namespace can arrange access to any Secret there. Encryption at rest does not fix that authorized access path or protect repository copies. Sources: [secret.md, lines 35–48 and 285–307](/private/tmp/doktimoi-bench-1-competitor-sWyioT/docs/concepts/configuration/secret.md:35).

9. **StorageClass policy is not a retroactive PV migration.** Dynamically provisioned PVs inherit their class’s reclaim policy, defaulting to `Delete`; existing PVs require individual editing or patching. Do not assume changing the class changes existing volumes. `Retain` preserves the released PV and backing data for manual reclamation after PVC deletion. Supported `Delete` behavior removes both PV and backing storage. In-use PVC deletion is postponed.  
   StatefulSet deletion/scaling is separate: PVC retention defaults to `Retain`; optional `whenDeleted` and `whenScaled` policies can delete claims after affected Pods terminate, subject to the documented feature prerequisite. Inspect both claim-retention and PV-reclamation settings. Sources: [persistent-volumes.md, lines 133–136 and 178–210](/private/tmp/doktimoi-bench-1-competitor-sWyioT/docs/concepts/storage/persistent-volumes.md:178); [statefulset.md, lines 387–423](/private/tmp/doktimoi-bench-1-competitor-sWyioT/docs/concepts/workloads/controllers/statefulset.md:387).

10. **Prioritize staged validation.** First, remove repository credential exposure, verify encryption/RBAC, enforce network policies, and inspect existing PV retention. Second, deploy measured probes, resource settings, draining, and grace periods. Third, enable explicit spreading and the stricter rollout strategy; exercise rollout, eviction-based drain, node failure, zone loss, and missing-metric scenarios while observing requests and readiness. These checks follow the distinct mechanisms documented above.

    Production acceptance still requires application or cluster evidence for request completion during shutdown, probe correctness, CPU-target suitability, spare scheduling capacity, CNI enforcement, and database recovery/durability. Dynamic provisioning itself establishes none of the database’s recovery objectives. For topology-constrained storage, evaluate `WaitForFirstConsumer`; its benefit depends on driver support.
